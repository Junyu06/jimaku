# Let translations follow one another on the page

On the translation row a translation follows the one before it on the same line and runs on to the next line, like the sentences of a paragraph. Nothing is put between two translations; between Latin letters there is a space. When a translation does not fit in what is left of the page, the page is cleared and it starts the new page at the top.

Why: the owner wanted more of what was said in the window. With a line for each sentence, a turn of two or three characters took a whole line that holds more than twenty, and a page of five lines was full after five short turns. Running on, a page holds several times as much, and the marks the translation already has, full stops and commas, show where one sentence ends.

Tried and dropped the same day: a gap between translations, as wide as a Chinese character, to show where one thing said ends when it has no mark at its end. On a physical iPad the owner found the gaps made the page look untidy and asked for none.

Cost accepted:

- Two short turns that the translation gives no mark, by different people, read as one phrase.
- The beginning of a translation is no longer at the beginning of a line, so finding the newest one on a page that is filling takes a look at where the text ended before.
- A page that is turned for a translation that does not fit leaves the rest of its last line, or more, unused.

Status of evidence: the row and the board are covered by unit tests. Running on with a gap was seen on a physical iPad by the owner; running on without one has only been seen in an iPad simulator.
