# Ask the recognizer on a short quiet and hold its answer until the pause is certain

The recognizer is asked to finalize as soon as speech has been followed by 0.2 seconds of quiet, a possible pause. What it returns is not a caption yet. It is passed on as speech in progress, and its translation is started in the background. When the quiet has lasted the 0.3 seconds that make a pause certain (0.2 after long speech, as before), what was held becomes the caption, and its translation is usually done or nearly done.

If the speaker goes on before the pause is certain, the short quiet was a breath in the middle of a sentence. What was held stays in front of what is said next, and the two end as one caption at the next certain pause. The full stop the recognizer put at the end of the first part is taken off; other marks stay. The translation started for the first part is not used.

- A final result is held if it ends after the last certain pause and no later than 0.05 seconds after the possible pause asked about. A result that ends before a pause already certain, or that the recognizer gave by itself while speech went on, is final at once, together with anything held before it.
- When a pause is certain while the recognizer still owes its answer to the last possible pause, what is held waits for that answer and ends with it, as one piece. If the answer has not come after 0.3 seconds more of audio, and the recognizer has reported no speech it has not finalized, what is held ends without it; that is what happens after a knock.
- An answer that ends more than 0.16 seconds before the last possible pause asked about is the answer to an older one. The last one is then still owed its answer. The older answer is held, to end with the one still owed, unless the speaker had stopped for certain between the two; then it is a sentence of its own and final at once.
- The app looks at the incoming audio about thirty times a second instead of ten, so a pause is noticed up to 0.07 seconds sooner.
- A translation is asked for by its text and made once, whether it was started ahead or when the caption was kept. Translations run one at a time; a new translator or a new session starts afresh and does not wait for a call of the old one.

Why: the owner found captions a little slow on a physical iPad: a translation often appeared when the next speaker had already begun. A logged session there showed where the time after a speaker stops went: 0.3 seconds waiting for the pause to be certain, up to 0.1 until the app next looked at the audio, about 0.05 for the recognizer, and about 0.18 for the translation, 0.53 seconds in the middle case. Starting the recognizer and the translation inside the wait takes the wait out of the sum.

Rejected: taking 0.2 seconds of quiet as a certain pause everywhere. On a Mac, over a recording of quick Japanese conversation, it was as fast and cost little recognition accuracy, but it cut 11 more captions out of 154 seconds, several in the middle of a sentence, such as a connective apart from its clause. Half a sentence does not translate.

Rejected: asking after 0.15 seconds. Over the same recording the characters recognized that matched a reference transcript fell from 93.5 to 92.0 percent; asked after 0.2 seconds they were 93.1 percent. A recognizer that is made to finalize in the middle of a sentence has less to go on for the words on either side.

Cost accepted:

- Now and then the recognizer answers after the pause is already certain: 3 times in 228 seconds on the iPad. In the build that session ran, what was held was then one caption and the late answer another. The rules above for answers that come late were added afterwards; a second session ran with an earlier form of them, and their present form has not run on a device.
- After a knock or another sound the recognizer has no words for, a caption that was being held comes 0.3 seconds later than it would have.
- A sentence finalized in two parts is recognized as two parts, each without the other as context, though it is translated as one.
- Translations started for a first part that the speaker then went on from are wasted work, and one that is still running delays the next by its remainder.
- While speech is held it shows on the original row as speech in progress, dimmer, a fraction of a second longer than before.

Status of evidence: the pause detector, the holding rules, and the store of translations are covered by unit tests. On a physical iPad, a logged 228-second session over a short-video app with quick Japanese conversation, with a build that held and joined as described but had none of the three rules for late answers named under "Cost accepted": the recognizer was asked at 111 possible pauses and gave 102 final results; for the 101 of them that came within a second of a request, the answer took 0.05 seconds in the middle case, 0.07 or less for nine in ten, and over 0.1 once. 83 of the 102 were held. 9 captions were made of two parts each. The letters and digits the recognizer finalized were the letters and digits of the captions, in the same order, none twice and none missing; marks and spaces were not compared. A translation was ready 0.42 seconds after the speaker stopped in the middle case (0.50 or less for nine in ten, 0.57 at most), for the 75 captions kept at a certain pause, where an earlier session the same day had 0.53 (0.89 at most). The owner reported this build as faster. The audio of that session was not copied off the device, so the nine joined captions were not checked against what was said.

A second logged session of 153 seconds, the same evening, ran a build that already held what was held while an answer was owed for speech the recognizer had reported, and took an answer to a pause already certain as final at once. The recognizer answered in 0.05 seconds in the middle case and 0.07 at most, for 70 final results; 60 were held; 11 captions were made of more than one part; three times a certain pause waited for an owed answer, which then came. Letters and digits again matched between what was finalized and the captions. A translation was ready 0.42 seconds after the speaker stopped in the middle case, 0.49 or less for nine in ten, 0.62 at most. The present rules, which wait for any owed answer up to 0.3 seconds and tell an older answer from the last, have not run on a device.
